[h3] A Tampa CPA Discusses Electronic Discovery for Small Businesses
[p] Large corporations are not the only entities that should be prepared to produce electronically stored information (ESI) in response to a lawsuit or a regulatory investigation. If anything, it’s even more important for small businesses to stay on top of potential electronic discovery (e-discovery) issues, because one seemingly minor records omission could have devastating repercussions. Big businesses might be able to weather a major setback, but a small business might find itself struggling for its very existence if e-discovery goes awry during a legal confrontation.

[p] This includes educating our clients about how best to keep orderly, easily accessed financial records – because few businesses avoid legal or regulatory entanglements of some sort. The types of records that might be relevant to e-discovery include:
[ul]
- E-mail
- Instant messages or chats
- Accounting databases
- Computer-aided design (CAD) and computer-aided manufacturing (CAM) files
- Websites
- Metadata
- Data from “smart” phones or personal digital assistants (PDAs)
- Any electronically stored information that could become relevant in a law suit
