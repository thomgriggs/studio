[h3] A Tampa CPA Explains the Meaning of Passive Income
[img] alt="Passive Income" src=http://www.reliancecpa.com/wp-content/uploads/passive-income-300x200.jpg
[p] The Internal Revenue Service generally divides income into three main categories: active, portfolio, and passive. Of these, passive income may be the most difficult to discern for people who are not extremely well-versed in tax law. Active income generally is considered money earned through work. Portfolio income generally is considered money that comes in via dividends, capital gains, interest, and other investments. In layman’s terms, passive income has come to mean revenue streams generated through non-active sources – there are many get-rich-quick schemes now based on “passive” income generated through Internet-based business activity. This is not quite in keeping with the IRS definition, which encompasses income sources such as rental property. Income from certain websites might also qualify, but the important thing to note about passive income is that it is taxable.
