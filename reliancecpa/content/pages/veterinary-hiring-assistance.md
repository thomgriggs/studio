[h2] Is It Time to Hire an Associate?
[p] Expanding your team is not a decision you should undertake lightly. Adding a new associate is a major investment. It also involves a personal commitment because you will need to adapt your working habits. The following are signs that it may be a good time to hire a new associate:
[ul]
- You have the financial resources necessary to expand your team
- You want to serve more clients or offer extended hours
- You want to add to your provided services and/or offer specialized treatments
- Your practice currently has a full schedule
[p] If you are unsure about your ability to hire an associate, consider adding a part-time employee. He or she will allow you to expand your practice with less of a financial investment.
[h2] Creating a Job Description
[p] Once you have decided to proceed with the hiring process, you will want to attract the right candidates. Writing an accurate job description is a vital step. Your job description should be concise and to-the-point. At the same time, it should contain certain essential information, including:
[ul]
- Job title
- Basic duties, procedures, and methods used
- Specialties or areas of expertise, if applicable
- Relationship to the overall practice structure
[h2] Employee Benefits
[p] When you are hiring a new associate, you will also need to establish employee benefits. Certain benefits are required by law. These include Social Security, unemployment insurance, disability insurance, and leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act. If you already have one or more employees, the structures for these benefits should already be in place. However, if you are hiring your first team member, you will need to apply for an Employer Identification Number (EIN), set up a payroll system, and complete any necessary paperwork.
[p] Although other benefits are optional, a good package will attract a larger pool of candidates. Then you will be able to choose the team members that are most suitable for your practice. In addition, this will create a nurturing and supporting work environment in which your team can grow. Non-required benefits include:
[ul]
- **Retirement plans and pensions:** There are a number of plans developed specifically for small business owners.
- **COBRA (Consolidated Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act) Benefits:** These benefits give employees and their families temporary access to health care if they retire or are laid off.
- **Employee incentive programs:** Small perks can make a huge difference in workplace morale. Incentives could include gym or recreational memberships, flexible working hours, and company functions, among others.
