The Role of a Personal Representative in Your Estate

By Danielle L. Vaughn, Esq., B.C.S., LL.M.

One of the most important decisions you make when creating an estate plan is choosing a personal representative, also known as an executor. This individual is responsible for carrying out your wishes after you pass away. Understanding this role can help you select the right person and ensure your estate is handled smoothly.

A personal representative manages the administrative tasks required to settle an estate. These duties typically include filing the will with the probate court, notifying beneficiaries and creditors, managing estate assets, paying debts and taxes, and ultimately distributing property according to the terms of the will. The personal representative acts as the person responsible for ensuring that the estate is properly administered under Florida law.

When choosing someone for this role, trust and reliability are key. Many people name a spouse, adult child, or close friend who is organized and capable of handling paperwork and communication. In more complex situations, some individuals choose to appoint a professional such as an attorney or trust company.

Before naming someone, it is important to discuss the responsibility with them. Serving as a personal representative can take months and often involves working with attorneys, accountants, and the probate court. Making sure the person is willing and able to serve can prevent complications later.

Selecting the right personal representative is one of the most important steps in estate planning. With thoughtful planning, you can ensure your wishes are carried out and your loved ones are supported.
