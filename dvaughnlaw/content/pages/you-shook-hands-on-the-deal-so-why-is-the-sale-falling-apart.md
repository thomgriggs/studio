[h1] You Shook Hands on the Deal. So Why Is the Sale Falling Apart?
[p] **By Danielle L. Vaughn, Esq., Board Certified Real Estate Attorney**
[p] You found the perfect Florida home. The seller accepted your offer. Everyone’s smiling and then, somewhere between contract and closing, everything unravels. Sound familiar? You’re not alone.
[p] In Florida, a real estate contract is legally binding the moment both parties sign, but that doesn’t mean the path to closing is smooth. Hidden title defects, unresolved liens, unpermitted renovations, and last-minute financing issues are among the most common deal-killers, and they can blindside even experienced buyers and sellers.
[p] Here’s what most people don’t know: Florida law gives buyers specific rights during the inspection and due diligence period, but those rights have strict deadlines. Miss a window, and you could lose your deposit, be locked into a bad deal, or worse, inherit someone else’s legal problems along with the property.
[p] ******An unpermitted addition or an old lien can follow a property for years, and become the new owner’s problem the moment the deed is signed.******
[p] A real estate attorney reviews your contract before you’re bound by it, spots red flags in the title search, and makes sure every contingency is in your favor. It’s the difference between a closing day celebration and a courtroom.
[p] Whether you’re buying your first beach bungalow or your fifth investment property, having legal counsel in your corner isn’t a luxury, it’s protection.
