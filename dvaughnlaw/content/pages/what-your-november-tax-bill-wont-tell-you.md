[h1] What Your November Tax Bill Won’t Tell You
[p] *The discount, the deadline, and the deed that can undo both*
[p] **By Danielle L. Vaughn, Board Certified Real Estate Attorney**
[p] Your Pinellas County property tax notice goes in the mail by November 1. Most of us glance at the number, wince, and pay it. Try reading it instead.
[p] Pay in November and you take 4 percent off. December drops to 3 percent, January 2, February 1, and by March you are paying full freight. Taxes go delinquent April 1. On a $9,000 bill, that November discount is $360, which buys a very good dinner on Corey Avenue.
[p] The discount is not the only reason to open the envelope. Look at the exemptions line.
[p] If you bought your home this year, the seller’s homestead exemption comes off the rolls December 31. It does not ride along with the deed. You must file your own application by March 1, and if you miss it, next year’s bill arrives without the exemption and without the Save Our Homes cap that limits how fast your assessment can climb.
[p] *“Homestead doesn’t come with the house. It comes with the paperwork.”*
[p] Sold a Florida homestead and bought another? Portability lets you carry up to $500,000 of accumulated Save Our Homes savings to the new home, but only if you apply for it, and only within a limited window after you leave the old one. Buyers move, file for homestead, and never claim the portability they were owed. That is a permanent loss, not a late fee.
[p] Here is the one that catches longtime owners. Deeds get changed casually: adding an adult child to the title, moving the house into an LLC, signing a trust transfer downloaded on a Sunday afternoon. Done wrong, that signature can strip the homestead exemption and reset a Save Our Homes cap you have been building for twenty years. The bill that follows is not a small surprise.
[p] So when the notice arrives, check three lines. Is the homestead exemption there? Is the assessed value noticeably below the market value, which is how you know the cap is working? Are the other exemptions you qualify for, senior, veteran, widow and widower, actually applied?
[p] A deed is a short document capable of costing you five figures a year. It deserves twenty minutes of a lawyer’s attention before you sign it, not after the bill shows up.
