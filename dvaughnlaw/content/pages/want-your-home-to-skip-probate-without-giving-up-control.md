[h1] Want Your Home to Skip Probate, Without Giving Up Control?
[p] **By Danielle L. Vaughn, Esq., Board Certified Real Estate Attorney**
[p] For most Floridians, the family home is their most valuable asset, and the one most likely to get stuck in probate. Many people try to avoid that by adding a child to the deed. It feels simple, but it can be a costly mistake: you’ve just given away part of your home, exposed it to your child’s creditors and divorces, and possibly triggered tax problems.
[p] Florida offers a better tool that most homeowners have never heard of: the Lady Bird deed, formally called an enhanced life estate deed.
[p] Here’s how it works. You keep complete control of your home for life. You can sell it, mortgage it, rent it out, or change your mind at any time, no one’s permission needed, and no one else’s signature required. When you pass away, the home transfers automatically to whomever you’ve named, with no probate, no court, and no waiting.
[p] But wait, doesn’t a will do the same thing? Not quite. A will tells the probate court where you want your property to go, but your family still has to go through probate to get there. That process can take months and cost thousands in fees. A Lady Bird deed bypasses the courthouse entirely: your beneficiaries simply record a death certificate, and the home is theirs.
[p] The benefits go further. A Lady Bird deed doesn’t disturb your homestead protection or property tax exemptions, and it’s one of the few tools that works alongside Medicaid planning. Because the transfer happens at death rather than as a lifetime gift, your beneficiaries also receive the property at its current market value for tax purposes, which can mean major capital gains savings if they later sell. Only a handful of states allow it, Florida is one of them.
[p] There’s flexibility, too. You can name multiple beneficiaries. And because the people you name have no legal rights to the property while you’re alive, you owe them nothing if you change your mind. Sell the home, and the deed simply has nothing left to transfer, no permission slips, no hurt feelings with legal consequences.
[p] Compared to other estate planning tools, it’s also remarkably affordable. A properly drafted Lady Bird deed costs a fraction of what a revocable living trust does, making it one of the best values in Florida estate planning for homeowners whose main asset is the house itself.
[p] It isn’t right for everyone. Complex family situations, blended families, or multiple properties may call for a trust instead. And a poorly drafted deed can create the very title problems it was meant to avoid, this is not a do-it-yourself project.
