The documents, in plain language

Wills and trusts

A will records wishes about your estate and identifies who you want to handle it. A trust can serve a different role in managing and transferring property. Choosing an approach starts with what you own, your family circumstances, and your goals.

Bring your existing documents if you have them. We can discuss whether they still reflect your wishes, what has changed, and the questions to resolve before drafting or updating a plan.

After a loss

Probate

Probate is a court process for administering a person’s estate after death. Whether it is needed, and what the process involves, depends on the estate and the way property was held.

We help families begin with the available documents, the property involved, and the immediate questions. Tell us if the person lived outside Florida but owned property here, or if family members disagree about an inherited home.

Planning during your lifetime

Powers of attorney
and health care

These documents address who can help with financial and health-care decisions and how you want your wishes recorded.

Think about the people you trust, whether they are willing to take on that responsibility, and what you want them to understand. Bring questions about powers of attorney, health-care surrogates, and living wills to the same conversation.

Where real estate and estate planning meet

Your home is part of the plan

For many families, the home is their largest asset. How it is titled matters to the plan you put in place. Ask whether a Lady Bird deed, a trust, or another approach fits your circumstances.

Our real estate and estate-planning practices bring those questions together. Bring your deed and existing planning documents so ownership and future wishes can be considered together.

Just moved to Florida?

A move is a good reason to review an existing plan. Ask about your documents, property ownership, homestead, and any changes in your family or priorities.

For business owners

Business succession

Your plan may need to address more than personal property. Discuss who would manage the business, how ownership would be handled, and how business documents work with your estate plan.
