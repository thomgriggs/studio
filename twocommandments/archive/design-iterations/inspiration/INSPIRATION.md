# II Commandments — inspiration

Source: the founder's Google Doc "CALLING ALL CHRISTIANS" (Design Reference Points // Flow, Cinematic Websites, Store Related), read 2026-09-30. His own comments are quoted; my notes say what each reference points to for *our* screens. Screenshots in `screens/` are static captures — several of these sites are WebGL experiences behind a loader or an "enter" gate, so the screenshot shows the door, not the room; the notes describe what's inside.

The site has two halves and the references split the same way: **the message** (storytelling / cinematic) and **the store** (product sites he loves).

---

## The message — storytelling references

### Nomadic Tribe — makemepulse (2019) · https://2019.makemepulse.com/
> "Great storytelling site. I like how it leads you from one part of the story to another. Def a reference point for us."

`screens/nomadic-tribe-makemepulse.jpg` (the entry circle). A scroll-driven WebGL journey: one continuous scene, the camera moves as you scroll, text arrives in short beats, each chapter hands you to the next. **Points to:** the home page as one uninterrupted scroll, panel → panel with no hard cuts; short copy per beat; a progress sense of "where am I in the story".

### Benno's Light · https://bennos-light.org/
> "Once you're 'in', click on 'Chapter Content'. I like that set up for row 11 on the excel 'related verses after'. Something to consider. I like the way it animates when you click on 'All Chapters'."

`screens/bennos-light-entry.jpg`. A testimony told in chapters, with a chapter index you can open at any time; the index animates open over the experience. **Points to:** the related verses (panel 11) as a *chapter index* — a side track you open, not another panel you scroll through; and the four Gospels as chapters.

### atterwasch — a scroll-documentary · https://www.atterwasch.net/
> "Possibly the best example of carrying a narrative."

`screens/atterwasch-hero.jpg`, `screens/atterwasch-chapters.jpg`. Full-bleed black-and-white photography, a serif wordmark set *on* the image, then a stark black chapter list (Future / Present / Episode 1–5 / Past). **Points to:** Jesus imagery as full-bleed photography with the verse set on it (panel 1); restraint — one typeface, one image, one line; chapters as a plain list.

### An interactive adventure by Pierre Hermé & Nicolas Buffe · http://pierrehermenicolasbuffe.com/en/chapter-1
`screens/pierre-herme-loader.jpg`. Illustrated chapters, a loader that is itself a drawing. **Points to:** the II Commandments mark could be the loader/transition device (panel 2, "layered designs").

### The Legend of Santar — ThreeSixtyEight · https://santar.webflow.io/
> "Highly animated."

`screens/legend-of-santar-begin.jpg`. A **Begin** gate with "best experienced with sound", then a scroll-animated tale in Webflow — proof this can be done without WebGL. **Points to:** an explicit start (the "momentary experience when you reach the site"); Webflow-style scroll animation is enough.

### Every Last Drop · https://everylastdrop.co.uk/
> "Keeps you engaged through the whole story."

`screens/every-last-drop-scroll.jpg`. Flat illustration, a day in a life, the scene changes as you scroll; sky colour shifts with the time of day. **Points to:** background/colour changing per panel so scrolling *feels* like moving through a day (or through the Gospels).

### Slavery Footprint · https://slaveryfootprint.org/
> "Still my favorite even though it doesn't scroll like it used to."

`screens/slavery-footprint-hero.jpg`. One question in a serif, centered, two choices, an arrow down. **Points to:** the *tone* of the hero — a single line, generous space, one thing to do next. This is the closest thing on his list to what panel 1 should feel like.

## The store — product-site references

## What the references agree on
1. **One thing on screen at a time.** Slavery Footprint, sofi, atterwasch: a single line, huge, with air. That's panel 1 and panel 3.
2. **It's a scroll-story, not a page.** Nomadic Tribe, Every Last Drop, atterwasch: continuous, chaptered, background shifts with the chapter. The four Gospels are the chapters; each gets a colour world (De La Calle).
3. **An index you can open, not a wall you scroll.** Benno's Light "All Chapters" → related verses (panel 11) as a side drawer.
4. **Serif display + a working body face, on paper, not on black.** Graza and atterwasch both. (The current skeleton uses Fraunces + Inter on paper — close; the hero currently sits on black, which the references don't support except atterwasch's photography.)
5. **Designers get their own world.** Mack & Pouya, De La Calle: profile pages that take the designer's palette and voice.
6. **Products live in the story.** Graza's product-page-as-story, Ghia's strewn products, Bite's card menu for church/designer.

## What's missing from his list
- Nothing here is *religious*. The tone is set by beauty and storytelling, not church aesthetics — that's a deliberate choice worth confirming with him.
- No mobile references. Every one of these is a desktop showpiece; the merch buyers will be on phones.
- No reference for the Bible-version picker itself; ewtn.com/bible (from the sheet) is the closest.
